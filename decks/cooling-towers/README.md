### Slide 3: Cooling Tower Types - Overview
- Distinguished by:
  - Draft: natural (chimney/stack) or mechanical (fans)

  - Heat Dissipation: wet (contact), wet-dry, or dry (surface)
  - Application: power or industrial
- Designs:
- Natural-Draft Cooling Towers
  - Hyperbolic Towers
- Mechanical-Draft Cooling Towers
- Atmospheric Spray Towers
- Deck-Filled Towers
- Spray-Filled Towers
- Coil Shed Towers

### Slide 4: Natural Draft Cooling Towers
- Natural draft, crossflow, contact tower
- Utilizes chimney effect for air movement
- These Large devices have high heat rejection
- Most common is Hyperbolic tower used in power plant applications

### Slide 5: Natural Draft Tower History
- First designs were spray ponds in which air traveled horizontally through louvers, contacted by upflowing spray.
- Next designs implement counterflowing, vertical draft with downflowing air
  - This increases residence time and loading by reducing velocities
- Packing was installed to increase wetted area
- Towers were arranged in rows to reduce effect of ambient winds

### Slide 6: Hyperbolic Cooling Tower Overview
- Early designs consisted of cylindrical configuration, then truncated cones, and now hyperbolic shape
- Performance characterized by duty coefficient, CD
- (Natural) draft is due to density difference in air and the uplifting wind on effluent (for up-flowing air)
- Increases in wetted area, cooling range, and humidity all improve CD
- Counterflow is more efficient than crossflow arrangement due larger ∆Tlm  and head of down pouring water (results in higher dP across columns 

### Slide 7: Mechanical Draft Cooling Towers
- These designs: Mechanical Draft, crossflow or counterflow, contact towers
  - Flow orientation options 
-  Air Draft can either be:
  - Indirect/Induced: pulled by fan
  - Forced: pushed by blower
    - Higher power requirement, only necessary greater differential pressure climb

### Slide 8: Atmospheric Spray Towers
- Natural draft, cocurrent, contact tower
- Effectiveness depends on atmosphere and nozzle design.
- Typically, lower efficiency but lower maintenance

### Slide 9: Additional Types
- Deck-Filled Cooling Towers: splash bars breakup water
- Spray-Filled Towers: spray nozzles for water breakup
- Coil-Shed Towers: additional coils placed underneath for further cooling of water effluent 

### Slide 10: Cooling Tower Jargon (1/2)
- Acceptance Testing determines operating cooling capacity
- Ambient Dry-/Wet-Bulb Temperatures are the temperatures of a thermometer bulb that represent the actual air temperature and the temperature at which air can be cooled to, respectively.
  - Approach to Wet Bulb is the ∆T of the cooled air effluent and the WBT
- Blowdown/Purge is the intentional disposal of circulating water to avoid buildup of contaminants
- Capacity refers to the cooling water flow
- Cell – cooling tower unit defined by wall encapsulation
- Cooling Factor is the ratio of water flow to air flow
- Delta/Range is the temperature different of water
- Drift is the entrained water droplets that escape with air effluent (% of water)

### Slide 11: Cooling Tower Jargon (2/2)
- Fog is the cloudy plume of the air effluent that forms with significant air heat rise and colder ambient air presence that descends to ground level
  - Has the potential to ice nearby roads
- Makeup is the water flow required (% of water circulated) to substitute lost cooling water due to evaporation, drift, blowdown, and leakage
- Net Effective Volume is the portion (% volume) of the tower where contact of air/water is occurring
- Performance is measured as the cooling water volumetric flow for designated range at designated wet-bulb temperature
  - Performance Curve 
- Psychrometer is an instrument to measure wet bulb temperature
- TDS = total dissolved solids, the amount of contaminant present
- Windage is the loss of air intake due to malfunctioning of upstream wind check walls
  - Wind check walls are barriers that prevent perturbation of static air pressure

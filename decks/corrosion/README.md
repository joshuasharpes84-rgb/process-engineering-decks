### Slide 4: Fundamentals of Corrosion
- ASTM Definition: deterioration of material (usually metal) due to chemical/electrochemical reaction with environment
- Originally, Pure Metals are intentionally converted from crude minerals (ores) via smelting process;
  - Corrosion is thermodynamic reverse of this process, resulting in metal oxide product:
- There are both liquid- and gas-phase corrosion mechanisms, gaseous being more exclusive to higher temperatures
- Fundamentals of Corrosion

### Slide 5: Types of Corrosion
- Wet Corrosion (Liquid-phase) involves the dissolution of metal ions into the electrolyte. This is an electrochemical mechanism
  - Dependent on polarity of liquid;
    - Non-polar: Dielectric contestant < 10  Usually not corrosive.
    - Polar: increasing solubility things protection layers
  - Atmospheric Corrosion is common mechanism (humidity and rain)
- Dry Corrosion (Gas-phase) involves direct chemical reaction of gas forming oxide scale
- Fundamentals of Corrosion

### Slide 6: Electrochemistry of Corrosion
- Two reactions take place :
  - Anodic Reaction (Oxidation): atoms loses electrons, ions carry away
  - Cathodic Reaction (Reduction): metal loses electrons to electrolyte
  - Third reaction takes place in the case of halide presence (Cl- )
- Driving force of reaction is quantified by Grxn = -n*E*F
  - Larger potential (E): greater reaction rate (when no oxide/coating layer)
  - Question: why is G thermos driving force yet E is reaction rate?
- Fundamentals of Corrosion

### Slide 7: Pourbaix Diagram: Corrosion vs Passivation Illustration
- Plotting Potential (oxidizer concentration*) vs pH (process solution), we can visualize why acidic solutions leave metal more susceptible to corrosion:
- Corrosion leaves metal without elections
- Passivation forms solid oxide films
- *Oxidizer concentration is not only constituent of potential; but very impactful
- These are for each ion within the metal; each ion has different behavior for at given pH, V.
- Corrosion mechanisms usually involve only one ion of complex
- Fundamentals of Corrosion

### Slide 8: Polarization: Electron Driving Force

  - CR = (Ki * icorr  * EW)/ρ
    - where icorr = B/Rp,   (B=constant)
  - Passivation increases resistance towards polarization, lowering corrosion current density

### Slide 9: Generalized vs Localized Corrosion
- Generalized Corrosion occurs uniformly across surface

- Localized Corrosion is non-uniform

  - Crevice Corrosion
  - Intergranular Corrosion
  - Galvanic Corrosion
  - Dealloying Corrosion

### Slide 13: Galvanic Corrosion
- Metals have different anodic, cathodic potentials driving electron transport through medium liquid (electrolyte)
  - This imbalance results in 1 metal (anodic side) corroding while cathodic side stays intact
- Localized Corrosion Types

### Slide 14: Dealloying Corrosion
- Selective leaching where more electrochemically active metal dissolves in solution and is removed from complex.
- Results in geometrically identical structure w/ compromised strength
- Notable example is Cu-Zn Alloys (dezincification mechanism)
- Localized Corrosion Types

### Slide 16: Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC)
- Metal cracks due to simultaneous tensile stress + corrosive environment
  - Cracks are usually trans granular, sometimes partially intergranular 
- Most common is Austenitic (non-magnetic, FCC) Stainless steel in chloride environment (salt water)
  - Must select CS or Alloy for this service
- Environmentally Assisted Cracking Types

### Slide 17: Hydrogen Embrittlement (HE)
- Mechanism: Intergranular crack formation from loss of ductility/hardness
- H+  ions from sources of HF, H2S, and HCN result in Methane gas formation (rxn w/ Carbon) within metal
  - Gas molecules are trapped weaken metal
- Metals w/ high tensile strength, typically measured in hardness, are most susceptible
- Notable Incident: Heat exchanger failure, Anacortes refinery, 2010
- Environmentally Assisted Cracking Types

### Slide 18: Liquid Metal Embrittlement (LME)
- Hot, inviscid liquid penetrates metal grain boundaries forming intergranular fractures:
- Common Mechanisms (both don’t require corrosion):
  - Mercury  Alloys
  - Molten Zn/Cu  Austenitic Stainless Steel
- Environmentally Assisted Cracking Types

### Slide 19: Corrosion Fatigue (CF)
- Trans granular cracks result due to the persistent fatigue cycles in the presence of corrosion.
  - Notably lower fatigue threshold than standalone fatigue
- Common for systems experience mechanical vibration
- Environmentally Assisted Cracking Types

### Slide 20: Additional Forms of Corrosion
- Microbial Influenced Corrosion
  - Biological life fouls metal forming pits
  - Exact Mechanisms are numerous – greatest example is S-containing microbes on CS/SS resulting in Fe-S
- Fretting Corrosion
  - Contacting metals shift and break welds, resulting in passivation layer erosion (this is not a direct corrosion mechanism, per se)

### Slide 21: High Temperature Gaseous Corrosion
- Mechanisms dependent on corrosive species:
  - Oxidation (O2  from Air)
  - Carburization (C from CO, CH4)
  - Sulfidation (S from H2S, Mercaptans, and SOx )
  - Halogenation
  - Nitridation (N from NH3,  N2, NOx)
  - Molten Salts & Metals
- Generally, High Temperature Ni-Alloy is the default material w/ necessary resistance.
  - Cases should still be evaluated individually

### Slide 23: HTGC: Carburization
- Carbon from CO and CH4 are known to ingress into metal forming both intergranular, trans-granular cracks
  - Carburization is dependent on thermodynamic activity, ac (~0  no carburization, <1  carburization occurs, >1 carbon coke deposit forms which inhibits carburization)
- 3 main reactions: Reduction, Boudouard, and Hydrocarbon Thermal Cracking
- Best mitigations for Carburization are metal composition alterations; addition of Ni, Si, Al, Molybdenum, etc.
  - Iron-based alloys are highly susceptible
- High Temperature Gaseous Corrosion Types

### Slide 24: HTGC: Sulfidation
- Two main types of Sulfidation, both of which require O2  + SO2/SO3 environment:
  - Hot Corrosion: salt vapor (alkali sulfates: Metal + SOx) present w/ O2  forms deposit that destroys oxidation layer, especially at higher temperatures (Type I)
    - Ni-based alloys most resistant
  - Reducing (non-oxidizing) Sulfidation: O2 -lean environment + metal leads to destructive sulfide deposits
    - Co-base alloys are most resistant
- High Temperature Gaseous Corrosion Types

### Slide 25: HTGC: Halogens
- Chlorine and Fluorine are most common, w/ some cases of Bromine and Iodine
- Mechanism involves alloy elements reacting w/ halogens
- Most resistant metals are Ni-alloys and Titanium
  - O2  presence reverses compatibility

### Slide 26: HTGC: Nitridation
- Dissociated N from N2, NH3  adsorbs into metal forming metal-nitrides
  - Ammonia services: > 600C
  - Nitrogen services: >1000C
- Nickel-alloys are much more resistant than Iron-alloys
  - Typically, oxide scale is sustained unless thermal cycling takes place
- High Temperature Gaseous Corrosion Types

### Slide 27: HTGC: Molten Metals/Salts
- **A form of liquid phase corrosion; Gaseous environment destroys passivation film
- System consisting of Gas, Molten Median, and Containment Metal results in selective leaching of reactive elements out of containment alloy
- Corrosive molten media include:
  - Nitrates
  - Chlorides
  - Carbonates
  - Zinc
  - Sodium
- High Temperature Gaseous Corrosion Types

### Slide 29: Corrosion Prevention: Introduction + Strategies
- Carbon Steel is the default material w/ exceptions:
  - Temperatures > 800F  Ni-alloys required
  - Acids, molten salts, aqueous solutions are present
- Corrosion of material-metal pairings can be found through numerous sources:
- Best strategies, in order:
  - Increase thickness of material (corrosion allowance)
  - Clad/line the vulnerable material w/ resistant material
  - Upgrade material altogether
- Corrosion Prevention

### Slide 30: Corrosion Prevention: Heat Exchanger Design
- Numerous Problems exist; here are some wise design practices:
  - Avoid oversizing horizontal steam-shell applications, epically for 1-tube pass (local overheating of top tubes)
  - Vaporization in tubes should require material selection based on isolated salts (highest concentration)
  - Condensation in tubes: select material that can tolerate non-conveying buildup of salts in tube sheet headers (dead legs)
  - Limit overheating cooling water in SS service; can result in pitting, SCC
  - Condensation in shell needs adequate outlet nozzle to avoid slug fatigue
  - Fired Heaters should not allow condensation of steam diluent (violent vaporization); insulate appropriately

### Slide 31: Corrosion Prevention: Distillation Design
- Towers are normally designed to handle both ends of composition range.
- Unusual composition zones result from ionization/vaporization of components such as CO2, HCl in Water. These system are prone to accumulation of corrosive ions, (or components)
  - Mitigations: absorbent charge (NaOH for CO2  ) and side draw to neutralization treatment
- Corrosion Prevention

### Slide 32: Corrosion Prevention: Tank Design
- Tanks usually only have single solution  Material selection is simple
- However, there are some exceptions.
- Flat Bottom Tanks:
  - water phase separates below less dense organic phase w/ outlet nozzle elevated
    - Slow corrosion rate but increased timeframe
  - water builds up on soil-side due to lack of drainage
    - Cathodic protection systems
- Corrosion Prevention

### Slide 33: Corrosion Prevention: Piping Design
- Dead legs are the biggest concern. While inevitable, they can be reduced through measures:
  - Feed points should be downflowing
  - Minimize unnecessary length, fittings, elevation changes
  - Valves close to junction points
- Heat Exchanger bypass reconnection points are prone to thermal stressing: use Wye Tees
- Wye Tee
- Corrosion Prevention

### Slide 34: Methods for Altering Process Environment
- Passivation Promotion: oxidizer is added to supplement low film buildup
- Oxidizer Removal: for aqueous environments, use deaerators
- Scale Enhancers: Molybdates charge to add scale to aqueous solutions where oxidizer can not be removed
  - Cooling tower open to atmosphere
- Corrosion Prevention

### Slide 3: Introduction (Chapter 1)
- HAZOP team identifies meaningful deviations in the process that could lead to hazards, then assesses whether there are adequate measures in place to minimize the risk for this hazard
  - Hazard is defined as a physical situation with the potential for
    - human injury,
    - damage to property,
    - damage to environment
  - Risk is defined as the product of Severity and Likelihood of a hazard
- If there are not adequate measures in place, teams creates action item for more rigorous (quantitative) safety analysis  - e.g., LOPA

### Slide 12: Identifying Causes & Consequences of Meaningful Deviations  Determine Risk
- HAZOP is qualitative:
  - Causes & Consequences are both evaluated with order-of-magnitude estimates
  - These estimates are plugged into a risk matrix (existing safeguards ignored) to determine necessary risk reduction
  - When team finds unacceptable risk, existing safeguards are considered to see if further action is required

### Slide 13: HAZOP Proposed Actions  How to Proceed
- Two Extremes:
  - HAZOP Team can take the wheel, fixing the problem themselves with proposal/implementation of action
  - Alternatively, HAZOP team can completely defer this task to more quantitative assessment
- It is best for HAZOP team to do a mix of these: suggest action for LOPA teams' approval
  - This allows for continuity and well-rounded evaluation

### Slide 16: Organizing a HAZOP Study (Chapter 5)

### Slide 17: Defining the Scope and Objectives of the Study and Establishing the Boundaries
- HAZOP Scope/Definition include:
  - HSE Risks
  - Responsibilities (study initiation, action item follow-up, etc.)
  - Operability Problems
- HAZOP Boundaries include:
  - Batteries Limits (ISBL & OSBL)
  - Mode of Operation: Batch, Continuous, Start-up/Shut-down

### Slide 18: Assimilating HAZOP Team
- Leader: extensive knowledge of HAZOP process and team members is necessary for encouraging effective participation
- Scribe: word-for-word transcribing is necessary at scribe’s discretion, most conversations are paraphrased
- Members: number is proportional to size/complexity of project
  - Specialized ICs such as chemists, instrumentation, maintenance personal are often included
- Example HAZOP Team

### Slide 19: Preparation
- With boundaries (battery limits) established  Leader must define pathway
  - This is typically upstream-to-downstream; exchangers being an exception
- Pathway, viewed through P&IDs, involves sections/nodes comprised of individual items (vessels)
- Material and energy balances are used to establish entire operating envelope for HAZOP assessment:
  - Startup
  - Steady state
  - Turndown
  - Shutdown
- What about for Batch Units?  Pathway follows operating sequence steps
  - Items will need to be considered several times over

### Slide 20: Carrying Out a Study (Chapter 6)

### Slide 21: Premeeting With Client
- There is often outside representation present in these meetings, sometimes up to and including the facilitator
  - Adequate representation from all disciplines is ensured
  - Some members will leave positions – look for substitution
- Recent modifications need to be captured inside P&IDs
  - This gives “Approved for HAZOP” stamp
- Meeting room is critical:
  - Table shape should incentivize communication
  - Projection of materials should be accessible

### Slide 22: HAZOP Meeting Structure
- Consist of an initial and repeating study sessions
  - These meetings are all-day meetings throughout a week; best for continuity
  - Preparation must be given to members beforehand for guidewords/parameters/nodes that will be reviewed
- Process Engineer establishes design intent for operating envelope
- Action items must be approved by every team member regardless of individual assignment

### Slide 23: Recording and Auditing (Chapter 7)

### Slide 24: Purposes: Recording and Auditing
- Evaluations and subsequent action items are to be recorded for multiple purposes:
  - Action Item Execution
  - LOPA Basis
  - Management of Change Basis
  - Operator Training
  - HAZOP Auditing
- Thus, recordings should not only be done for adequate accounting within a meeting but for recollection of findings beyond it
  - Majority of stakeholders do not attend HAZOP meetings

### Slide 26: HAZOP Training (Chapter 8)

### Slide 27: Training
- Formal Training is typically given, either through 1-on-1 or online platforms (Example: AIChE Training)
  - This is best followed by participation in an actual study
- Experience of members is accelerated when Leader does not dominate thought processes; allows true participation
- Having experience through different functions gives well-rounded perspective
- Leader must not only contribute to their own right, but manage others to ensure members are developing competencies

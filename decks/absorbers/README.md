### Slide 2: Absorption Background

### Slide 4: Agitated Vessels
- Used primarily for batch processes as the continuous processes require multiple vessels for countercurrent operation
  - (continuous process) Single vessel would accumulate volume
- Very high pressure drop across vessel
- Great control of reaction in anticipation of byproduct formation.
- Notable Applications:
  - HCl/Water batch reactor
  - NH3/Water batch reacator

### Slide 5: Bubble Cap Absorbers
- These absorbers have higher residence times than that of packed towers. As such, This design is best for:
  - low liquid rates (packing would not be wetted)
  - High tower performance; operation nears equilibrium
  - Services with scaling potential (manhole cleaning accessibility)
  - Slower chemical reactions
- However, this design has much higher pressure drop which is why a combination of trays/packing is often used.
- Notable Applications:
  - SO2 and HCl absorption
- Physical Design: Trays with inverted cups that have slotted edges for vapor/liquid contact that lift upwards upon vapor flow. Inlet weirs dictate liquid distribution and dictate weirs control liquid level.

### Slide 6: Cooler Absorbers
- High heats of absorption required specialized heat removal
  - External cooler (standard)
  - Internal Cooling coils
  - Vessel Shell
  - Complete retrofit to shell and tube exchanger (absorption inside of tubes)
    - This is referred to as shell and tube exchangers.
- Materials of construction should have high thermal conductivity to accommodate heat load
  - For acid/water applications (highly exothermic), materials such as glass or impervious carbon are used to avoid corrosion.
- Notable Applications:
  - SO3, HCl, HF, and NO2 absorption

### Slide 7: Spray Towers
- Nozzles spray liquid into droplets which contact up flowing vapor.
- Should theoretically yield high surface areas but is much prone to entrainment or coalescence depending on gas velocities.
- Best for low absorption duties, low pressure drop requirements.
- Notable Applications:
  - SO2 removal from boiler exhaust
  - Quench towers
  - Odor control scrubbers

### Slide 8: Packed Towers (Wet Scrubbers)
- Most common type of absorber in which liquid is typically gravity drained countercurrent to up flowing vapor.
- Structured and random packing is installed to increase wetted area for maximum vapor/liquid contact with key considerations:
  - Pressure drop; a function of gas velocity — see below
  - Maldistribution; common with poor liquid distribution.
- Tower capacity is quantified with load and flood points on pressure drop vs gas velocity plot:
  - Load point is where liquid holdup begins to increase
  - Flood point is when entrainment into overhead occurs
  - Typically, towers operate between 40 and 70% of flood point

### Slide 9: Venturi Scrubbers
- These are effectively shortcut, one-stage scrubbers that have high pressured drops and low efficiencies.
  - Thus, they are only used gases with high solubilities
- Two types of Venturi designs:
  - Venturi scrubbers inject liquid into the throat of a gas stream which requires a downstream gas-liquid separator
  - Liquid-jet Eductors injects water orthogonal to gas to serve as absorbate knockdown agent
